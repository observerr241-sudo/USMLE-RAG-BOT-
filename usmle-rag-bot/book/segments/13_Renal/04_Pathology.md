### **Acute cystitis**  
Inflammation of urinary bladder. Presents as suprapubic pain, dysuria, urinary frequency, urgency. Systemic signs (eg, high fever, chills) are usually absent.  
Risk factors include female sex (short urethra), sexual intercourse, indwelling catheter, diabetes mellitus, impaired bladder emptying. Causes:  
- ▘ E coli (most common)  
- ▘ Staphylococcus saprophyticus—seen in sexually active young women (E coli is still more common in this group)  
- ▘ Klebsiella  
- ▘ Proteus mirabilis—urine has ammonia scent  
- ▘ Enterococcus spp.  
- Labs:<sup>⊕</sup> leukocyte esterase.<sup>⊕</sup> nitrites (indicates presence of Enterobacteriaceae). Sterile pyuria (pyuria with<sup>⊝</sup> urine cultures) could suggest urethritis by Neisseria gonorrhoeae or Chlamydia trachomatis.

### **Chronic pyelonephritis**  
**<mark>B</mark>**

The result of recurrent or inadequately treated episodes of acute pyelonephritis.
